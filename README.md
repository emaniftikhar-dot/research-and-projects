### 2. Segmenting the Sacred Marketplace: Demographic & Psychographic Predictors of Religious Scholar Selection
* **Type:** Advanced Independent Quantitative Research Study | Department of Management Sciences, LUMS
* **Methodology:** Cross-sectional quantitative survey (n=114, urban educated cohort), K-Means Cluster Analysis (k=3 solution), Silhouette Diagnostics, OLS Regression, Cronbach's Alpha Reliability Testing, Spearman Correlation Matrices, and Mann-Whitney U Tests.
* **Theoretical Integrations:** Source Credibility Theory, Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), Parasocial Interaction Theory (PSI), Digital Governmentality, Brand Transgression & Moral Decoupling.
* **Key Findings:** 
  * **Gender as Primary Fault Line:** Statistically significant gender consumption gaps across 9 of 18 evaluated public scholars (p < 0.05).
  * **Post-Sectarian Algorithmic Shift:** Family sectarian affiliation (Maktab-e-Fikr) showed zero statistically significant association with digital consumption (χ² = 10.09, p = 0.433), proving that digital platforms cultivate personalized, post-denominational religious consumption.
  * **Cosmopolitan Identity Dynamics:** Cosmopolitanism operates as a negative predictor of local/subcultural scholar consumption (Sahil Adeem r = -0.39***; Tariq Jameel r = -0.26**) rather than merely amplifying global exposure.
  * **Audience Typologies:** Identified three distinct segments: *Cosmopolitan Female Seekers* (95.8% female, highest English preference, prioritizing women's leadership advocacy), *Traditional Male Engagers*, and *Low-Engagement Moderates*.
* 📄 `File:` `SegmentingtheSacredMarketplace_26110155_IndepStudy.docx`
